# 04 Visual artifact lifecycle

**Status:** resolved
**Blocked by:** none
**Type:** task

## Question

How should visual material be discovered, selected, and preserved without turning the vault into a
media archive or losing knowledge that prose and transcripts omit?

## Answer

Use visuals sparingly but proactively when they materially improve understanding. Appropriate
visuals include source screenshots that show diagrams, slides, comparisons, examples, or
demonstrations; reference photographs where visual distinction matters; and occasional generated
informational diagrams or infographics when they explain something more effectively than prose.
Do not add an image merely because one is available, and avoid decorative imagery and unnecessary
image saturation. Do not use visibly watermarked content.

The source-specific procedure owns inspection needed to discover visual evidence during
acquisition. In particular, YouTube acquisition must inspect regular-interval and scene-change
storyboards alongside the transcript rather than assuming the transcript describes every useful
visual. Its result includes candidate timestamps and coverage without deciding their place in the
vault.

Visual sourcing belongs to ingestion's acquisition state or curation's visual-preparation state.
It may preserve a screenshot, diagram, slide, comparison, example, or demonstration from supplied
or acquired material; capture a high-quality source-media frame; obtain a reference photograph;
create a generated informational visual; or correct a temporary copy of a candidate. It keeps
candidates temporary, and its result includes their purpose, source, timestamp, generation
provenance, corrections, and usage constraints without deciding that they belong in the vault.
Curation returns prepared candidates to integration for assessment. It switches to ingestion only
when an independent source must be preserved.

Visual assessment during integration separately decides whether an available candidate materially
improves understanding and is faithful, supported, contextualized, legible, suitable quality,
sufficiently sourced, and not needlessly duplicative. Assessment does not obtain, generate,
extract, edit, move, or replace the artifact. Once accepted, retain the artifact once in the
established attachment hierarchy with appropriate provenance and reuse it across notes.

Treat downloaded video, bulk sampled frames, scene-change frames, contact sheets, storyboards, and
unaccepted candidates as temporary procedure artifacts.

Clean temporary media after successful processing. Preserve it on failure only long enough to make
diagnosis or a safe retry possible.

## Evidence

During the skill audit, I approved a preparation route for curation so obtaining or correcting a
visual does not require introducing a source record. This replaces the original restriction of
visual sourcing to ingestion.

The original expanded draft required visual inspection and canonical reuse of valuable source
frames while keeping attachments in the vault's separate hierarchy. The requested pipeline also
creates potentially large intermediate video, frame, and contact-sheet files. The draft was
removed from the repository root after decomposition.

The draft explicitly calls for sparing but proactive use, distinguishes source screenshots,
reference photographs, and generated informational visuals, rejects decorative or saturated image
use, requires sufficient video inspection, and directs the agent to retain a high-quality source
frame only when it is useful.
